01

Abstract

Earth systems are local, physical, and unevenly observed. A general language model can talk about a river, an airshed, a soil, and a species in the same breath. It cannot be held to any of them. CARMA is a research programme to build small language models that stay inside a domain: Nabha for water, Vayu for air, Abha for land, and Indie for biodiversity.

Carbon is not given a fifth model. Carbon moves through water, air, land, and living systems, so it is read as a shared account across the four. The models are small on purpose. A small model can be trained, evaluated, and argued with by a research group. Its errors can be traced to a dataset, a place, and a question.

The names are working titles. Vayu keeps its meaning — wind. The others are assigned here and can move. The domains will not.

Keywords small language models, hydrology, atmosphere, land systems, biodiversity, carbon, earth observation

02

The argument

I

A useful model of the Earth is a model of a domain.

Nabha, Vayu, Abha, and Indie are separate so that each one can be tested by the science it claims. A model for water is judged on water. A model for biodiversity is judged on living systems. Fluency outside the domain is not evidence.

II

Small is a research decision, not a compromise.

The aim is a model a specialist can argue with, not a model that stands in for the specialist. Size is part of the method. A research group should be able to train the model, read it, repeat the work, and say where it fails.

III

Carbon is read across the models.

The four models stay apart so their errors stay interpretable. Carbon is where they are asked to speak to one another: the same element, four domains, one account. A single general climate model would blur that.

IV

Nothing on this page is a finding.

This is a prospectus. Training data, evaluations, and errors will be published with the models, as studies that name their places and their limits. Until then, the work is the argument and the questions below.

03

The models

Shown in Earth-system order. Each model has a written domain. Questions outside that domain are out of scope, not failures of fluency.

01

Nabha

Water

Working title

Nabha is a small language model of water. Its scope is the hydrosphere as it is measured and written: watersheds, water quality, storage, and the water cycle. The name comes from नभ, the sky that holds rain. People who work on water should be able to question it, and to see when it is wrong.

Watersheds · water quality · the water cycle

02

Vayu

Air

Working title

Vayu is wind. The word is already the right one, so the model keeps it. Vayu is a small language model of air: what the atmosphere holds, how it moves, and how air quality is observed and described. It is not a weather product and not a general assistant. It is a research instrument for the air, sized so its answers can be checked against measurements.

Atmosphere · air quality · the moving air

03

Abha

Land

Working title

Abha is a small language model of land. आभा is the light on a surface. The scope is soils, land cover, and land use — the ground as a physical system and as a record of how people live on it. Abha stays with the land. It does not speak for the species that live there. That is Indie’s work.

Soils · land cover · land use

04

Indie

Biodiversity

Working title

Indie is a small language model of biodiversity: species, habitats, and ecological communities. The name is a placeholder. It will be replaced when the research group chooses one that belongs to the science. Until then, Indie marks the part of the programme where living systems are studied on their own terms, rather than as a feature of a land or carbon model.

Species · habitats · ecological communities

Shared account

Carbon

Carbon has no model of its own in this thesis, and that is deliberate. It is stored and moved by water, air, land, and life. A fifth general model would blur the four domains in order to talk about all of them.

Each model is built so it can be asked a carbon question inside its own domain: carbon in water, carbon in air, carbon in soils and land use, carbon in living systems. The programme reads those answers together.

04

Open questions

  1. How small can a useful model be?

    What is the smallest model that still helps a hydrologist, an atmospheric scientist, a land scientist, or an ecologist — and where does further size stop changing the science?

  2. What is the model allowed to see?

    Which observations belong in the training of Nabha, Vayu, Abha, and Indie, and which must be held back so the model can be tested against a place it has not already memorised?

  3. How does carbon cross four models?

    How should a carbon question be asked of four separate models without forcing them into one voice, and how should the four answers be read when they disagree?

  4. What counts as an evaluation?

    A public test for this work has to be judged by a domain and a place, not by a general leaderboard. What does that record look like, and who is qualified to say the model failed?
